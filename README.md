Part One: Ways to Start Well
Principle 1: Begin with the decision, not the data
Every good piece of work starts with a question someone has to answer. Tell me what decision the work should support, and I will shape the maps, models or slides around it. Data comes second.
Principle 2: Say what success looks like
Before I open a file, I want to know who will read the result and what they will do with it. A flood manager, a donor and a student need very different outputs, even from the same model.
Principle 3: Share what you already have
Bring the data, reports, previous drafts and any constraints. Missing data is not a problem, but I need to know about it early so I can flag it before we build anything.
Principle 4: Agree the format before the work begins
Decide whether you need a map, a model, a report, a paper, a slide deck, a video or a mix. Naming the format early avoids the most common cause of revisions.
---
Part Two: Ways to Show That the Work Is Sound
Principle 5: Show the method, not only the result
A map or a flood depth figure is only as good as the steps behind it. I explain the assumptions, the software and the checks I used, so you can defend the result to others.
Principle 6: Check the data before the model
I review rainfall, river, groundwater and satellite inputs first and flag gaps or errors. This is cheaper than discovering a problem after the model is finished.
Principle 7: Make uncertainty visible
Good hydrology says how confident we can be. Where it matters, I show sensitivity tests, coherence checks, and ranges rather than a single number that looks more certain than it is.
Principle 8: Design for the reader
Slides lead with the message. Maps use one clear scale and plain labels. Reports answer the question in the first page. Videos get to the point quickly.
---
Part Three: Ways to Deliver Good Work Together
Principle 9: Work in visible stages
You receive a first draft early, with maps, figures or slides you can review. Feedback is then applied in a focused round, not a long chain of unrelated changes.
Principle 10: Respect ownership of data and ideas
Client data stays with the client. Unpublished academic work stays with its authors and supervisors. I share what I am allowed to share, and I say clearly when something cannot be shared.
Principle 11: Give people the files they need
Final deliverables come in the formats you asked for: GIS layers, PDFs, editable slides, Word documents or video files.
